# Visual Identity

Every color, face, mark, and badge the site draws with is declared in `palette.ts`, the stylesheets, `emblems.ts`, and the files under `site/public`. Each specimen below reads its values from those declarations as the site builds or as the page loads, so a change to a declaration reaches this page in the same change.

## Palette

The colors `palette.ts` declares sit in one band, grouped by the declaration that assigns each one. Choosing a strip names the color beneath the band beside its hex, token, and OKLCH value, its contrast against the page ground of each scheme, and every place the site reads it.

## Roles

A page draws with a small set of color roles, each a custom property the stylesheets read. The passage below sets every role in the scheme its switch names, and the legend beside it judges each role's contrast at the floor its level takes, a text role and a callout mark each against the page ground and a ground by the text it carries. Pointing at, focusing, or pinning a role marks every place the passage uses it.

<IdentityRoles />

## Typefaces

Each typographic role is set in the site's own styles and read back as the page loads. The index groups the roles by the face each one computes, and the pane gives the chosen role's weight, size, and tracking, the tokens and the selector that set them, a sample at its true size, the role marked in place among the others, and the face's figures and reference marks.

<IdentityTypefaces />

## Marks

The pen-nib P, the letters, and the tagline compose the lockup. The switches turn each part on or off, and the stage draws the parts turned on over the file's box, marking the clear space they leave on each side. The composed mark also sits in a copy of the nav title, where the corner handle or the ruler resizes it, and the diamond on the ruler marks the height the nav bar serves the mark at. The rows beneath name the file that draws the parts turned on and the tagline the lockup sets.

<IdentityMarks />

## Family Badges

Every rule family and each cross-cutting concern takes a badge drawn with a broad nib. Choosing a family draws its badge large, stroke by stroke, and the line beneath gives its motif, the ink it takes in the scheme the page is set to, its stroke count, and the number of rules the family holds.
